## 15.1.2.1. Methodological Meaning and Scope of Application

### 15.1.2.1.1. Econometric Foundation and Identification Mechanism

Avoids the curse of dimensionality via additive penalized B-splines E[Y|Z,X] = Z*gamma + sum_j f_j — X_j , reporting parametric policy effects alongside smooth Effective Degrees of Freedom — EDF — and nonlinearity Wald tests. Across empirical econometrics, `gam_semiparametric` enables researchers to isolate structural parameters while controlling for confounding covariates and verifying identification assumptions via `Stata 18 npregress` in Stata 18 MP, `mgcv` in R 4.x, and `statsmodels` in Python 3.11+.

### 15.1.2.1.2. Scope of Application and Sample Structure Requirements

Cross-sectional or panel regressions requiring flexible nonlinear controls — weather, age, firm size — alongside interpretable parametric policy covariates. Mandatory post-estimation verification includes testing identification restrictions, evaluating goodness-of-fit metrics, and confirming numerical convergence across all three econometric engines.

### 15.1.2.1.3. Structural Equations and Asymptotic Covariance Specification

Core structural equation for `gam_semiparametric`:

$$
g(\text{E}[Y_i \mid Z_i, X_i]) = Z_i^\top \gamma + \sum_{j=1}^p f_j(X_{ij}), \quad \min_{\gamma, f_j} \sum_{i=1}^N \mathcal{L}(Y_i, \eta_i) + \sum_{j=1}^p \lambda_j \int \left[f_j^\top ^\top (x_j)\right]^2 dx_j
$$

Estimator objective function and sample moment conditions:

$$
\hat{m}_h(x) = e_1^\top \left(\mathcal{X}_x^\top \mathcal{W}_h(x)\mathcal{X}_x\right)^{-1}\mathcal{X}_x^\top \mathcal{W}_h(x) Y, \quad h_{\text{opt}} = \arg\min_{h \gt 0}\text{CV}(h)
$$

Asymptotic covariance matrix and post-estimation diagnostic metric:

$$
T_N^\ast = \frac{\hat{\beta}_1^\ast - \hat{\beta}_1}{\text{SE}_{\text{CR}}(\hat{\beta}_1^\ast)}, \quad \text{CI}_{1-\alpha}^{\text{Boot}} = \left\{b : p_{\text{Boot}}(b) \ge \alpha\right\}
$$
