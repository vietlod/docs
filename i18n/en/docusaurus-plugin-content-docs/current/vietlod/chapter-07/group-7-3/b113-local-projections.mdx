## 7.3.1.1. Methodological Meaning and Scope of Application

### 7.3.1.1.1. Econometric Foundation and Identification Mechanism

Directly estimates horizon-by-horizon impulse responses without compounding VAR specification errors, remaining valid under persistent processes via lag augmentation. Across empirical econometrics, `local_projections` enables researchers to isolate structural parameters while controlling for confounding covariates and verifying identification assumptions via `Stata 18 lpirf` in Stata 18 MP, `lpirfs` in R 4.x, and `statsmodels` in Python 3.11+.

### 7.3.1.1.2. Scope of Application and Sample Structure Requirements

Macroeconomic or financial time series evaluating dynamic impulse responses to policy or price shocks across multiple horizons. Mandatory post-estimation verification includes testing identification restrictions, evaluating goodness-of-fit metrics, and confirming numerical convergence across all three econometric engines.

### 7.3.1.1.3. Structural Equations and Asymptotic Covariance Specification

Core structural equation for `local_projections`:

$$
Y_{t+h} = \alpha_h + \beta_h \text{shock}_t + \sum_{\ell=1}^p \Gamma_{h,\ell}^\top W_{t-\ell} + u_{t+h}, \quad \widehat{\text{IRF}}(h) = \hat{\beta}_h, \quad h = 0, 1, \dots, H
$$

Estimator objective function and sample moment conditions:

$$
Y_t = c + \sum_{\ell=1}^p A_\ell Y_{t-\ell} + B^{-1}\Sigma_u \varepsilon_t, \quad \Psi_h = \frac{\partial Y_{t+h}}{\partial \varepsilon_t^\top} = \Phi_h B^{-1}\Sigma_u
$$

Asymptotic covariance matrix and post-estimation diagnostic metric:

$$
\text{FEVD}_{j \leftarrow k}(H) = \frac{\sum_{h=0}^{H-1}\left(e_j^\top \Psi_h e_k\right)^2}{\sum_{h=0}^{H-1} e_j^\top \Psi_h \Psi_h^\top e_j}, \quad \det\left(I_K - \sum_{\ell=1}^p A_\ell z^\ell\right) \neq 0 \;\forall |z| \le 1
$$
