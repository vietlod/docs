## 1. Methodological Meaning and Scope of Application

### 1.1. Econometric Foundation and Identification Mechanism

Applies data-driven inverse-coefficient L1 weights to achieve Oracle support recovery without over-shrinking large signals. Across empirical econometrics, `adaptive_lasso` enables researchers to isolate structural parameters while controlling for confounding covariates and verifying identification assumptions via `Stata 18 lasso` in Stata 18 MP, `glmnet` in R 4.x, and `scikit-learn` in Python 3.11+.

### 1.2. Scope of Application and Sample Structure Requirements

Many correlated candidate predictors where standard Lasso suffers selection inconsistency. Mandatory post-estimation verification includes testing identification restrictions, evaluating goodness-of-fit metrics, and confirming numerical convergence across all three econometric engines.

### 1.3. Structural Equations and Asymptotic Covariance Specification

Core structural equation for `adaptive_lasso`:

$$
\hat{\beta}_{\text{AdaLasso}} = \arg\min_{\beta} \frac{1}{2N}\|Y - X\beta\|_2^2 + \lambda_N \sum_{j=1}^p \hat{w}_j |\beta_j|, \quad \hat{w}_j = \frac{1}{|\hat{\beta}_j^{\text{init}}|^\gamma}
$$

Estimator objective function and sample moment conditions:

$$
\hat{\beta}(\lambda, \alpha) = \arg\min_{\beta \in \mathbb{R}^p} \left\{\frac{1}{2N}\sum_{i=1}^N \left(Y_i - X_i^\top \beta\right)^2 + \lambda\left(\alpha \|\beta\|_1 + \frac{1-\alpha}{2}\|\beta\|_2^2\right)\right\}
$$

Asymptotic covariance matrix and post-estimation diagnostic metric:

$$
\text{CV}_K(\lambda) = \frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^K \frac{1}{|I_k|}\sum_{i \in I_k}\left(Y_i - X_i^\top \hat{\beta}^{(-k)}(\lambda)\right)^2, \quad \lambda_{\text{opt}} = \arg\min_{\lambda}\text{CV}_K(\lambda)
$$
