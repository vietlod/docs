## 1. Methodological Meaning and Scope of Application

### 1.1. Econometric Foundation and Identification Mechanism

Estimates the Huber Robust Linear Regression specification within the Robust and Quantile Regression framework by optimizing the structural objective function and computing heteroskedasticity- and cluster-robust asymptotic standard errors. Across empirical econometrics, `robust_rlm` enables researchers to isolate structural parameters while controlling for confounding covariates and verifying identification assumptions via `base Stata` in Stata 18 MP, `MASS` in R 4.x, and `statsmodels` in Python 3.11+.

### 1.2. Scope of Application and Sample Structure Requirements

Cross-sectional, pooled, or tabular microeconometric datasets requiring robust covariance estimation and specification testing. Mandatory post-estimation verification includes testing identification restrictions, evaluating goodness-of-fit metrics, and confirming numerical convergence across all three econometric engines.

### 1.3. Structural Equations and Asymptotic Covariance Specification

Core structural equation for `robust_rlm`:

$$
\hat{\beta}_{\text{RLM}} = \arg\min_{\beta} \sum_{i=1}^N \rho\left(\frac{Y_i - X_i^\top \beta}{\hat{\sigma}}\right)
$$

Estimator objective function and sample moment conditions:

$$
\hat{\beta}(\tau) = \arg\min_{\beta \in \mathbb{R}^K} \sum_{i=1}^N \rho_\tau\left(Y_i - X_i^\top \beta\right), \quad \rho_\tau(u) = u\left(\tau - \mathbb{I}(u \lt 0)\right)
$$

Asymptotic covariance matrix and post-estimation diagnostic metric:

$$
\widehat{\text{Var}}(\hat{\beta}(\tau)) = \frac{\tau(1-\tau)}{N}\left(\hat{J}_N(\tau)\right)^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N X_i X_i^\top\right)\left(\hat{J}_N(\tau)\right)^{-1}
$$
