## 3.1.5.1. Methodological Meaning and Scope of Application

### 3.1.5.1.1. Econometric Foundation and Identification Mechanism

Jointly estimates two correlated binary decisions or a binary outcome with an endogenous binary regressor via FIML. Across empirical econometrics, `bivariate_probit` enables researchers to isolate structural parameters while controlling for confounding covariates and verifying identification assumptions via `base Stata biprobit` in Stata 18 MP, `GJRM` in R 4.x, and `statsmodels / scipy` in Python 3.11+.

### 3.1.5.1.2. Scope of Application and Sample Structure Requirements

Two binary [0, 1] outcomes or one binary outcome and one endogenous binary treatment. Mandatory post-estimation verification includes testing identification restrictions, evaluating goodness-of-fit metrics, and confirming numerical convergence across all three econometric engines.

### 3.1.5.1.3. Structural Equations and Asymptotic Covariance Specification

Core structural equation for `bivariate_probit`:

$$
Y_{1i}^\ast = X_{1i}^\top \beta_1 + \gamma Y_{2i} + \varepsilon_{1i}, \quad Y_{2i}^\ast = X_{2i}^\top \beta_2 + \varepsilon_{2i}, \quad \begin{pmatrix}\varepsilon_{1i} \\ \varepsilon_{2i}\end{pmatrix} \sim \mathcal{N}_2\left(\begin{pmatrix}0\\0\end{pmatrix}, \begin{pmatrix}1 & \rho \\ \rho & 1\end{pmatrix}\right)
$$

Estimator objective function and sample moment conditions:

$$
\hat{\theta}_{\text{MLE}} = \arg\max_{\theta \in \Theta} \sum_{i=1}^N \sum_{j=1}^J y_{ij}\ln P_{ij}(X_i; \theta), \quad \frac{\partial \ell_N(\hat{\theta})}{\partial \theta} = \sum_{i=1}^N s_i(Y_i, X_i; \hat{\theta}) = 0
$$

Asymptotic covariance matrix and post-estimation diagnostic metric:

$$
\text{AME}_k = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N \frac{\partial \text{P}(Y_i = 1 \mid X_i; \hat{\theta})}{\partial X_{ik}}, \quad \text{LR} = 2\left(\ell_N(\hat{\theta}) - \ell_N(\tilde{\theta}_0)\right) \xrightarrow{d} \chi^2_{\text{df}}
$$
