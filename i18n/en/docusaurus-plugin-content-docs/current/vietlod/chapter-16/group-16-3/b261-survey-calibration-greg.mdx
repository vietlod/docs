## 16.3.2.1. Methodological Meaning and Scope of Application

### 16.3.2.1.1. Econometric Foundation and Identification Mechanism

Calibrates base design weights via minimum chi-square distance / exponential raking to match known population auxiliary totals, reducing nonresponse bias and design variance in Generalized Regression Estimation.

### 16.3.2.1.2. Scope of Application and Sample Structure Requirements

Complex surveys with auxiliary census benchmarks where post-stratification or Deville-Sarndal calibration stabilizes finite-population totals and regression coefficients.

- **Mandatory Pre-Estimation Diagnostics**: `survey_weight_positivity_precheck, vif_multicollinearity`
- **Mandatory Post-Estimation Diagnostics**: `survey_design_effect_deff, wald_linear_restriction`

### 16.3.2.1.3. Structural Equations and Asymptotic Covariance Specification

Core structural equation for `survey_calibration_greg`:

$$
Y_{ij} = X_{ij}^\top \beta + Z_{ij}^\top u_j + \varepsilon_{ij}, \quad u_j \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \Sigma_u), \quad \varepsilon_{ij} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_\varepsilon^2)
$$

Estimator objective function and sample moment conditions:

$$
\hat{\beta}_{\text{WLS}} = \left(\sum_{h=1}^H \sum_{c=1}^{C_h} \sum_{i=1}^{n_{hc}} w_{hci} X_{hci} X_{hci}^\top\right)^{-1} \left(\sum_{h=1}^H \sum_{c=1}^{C_h} \sum_{i=1}^{n_{hc}} w_{hci} X_{hci} Y_{hci}\right)
$$

Asymptotic covariance matrix and robust inference metric:

$$
\widehat{\text{Var}}_{\text{TSL}}(\hat{\beta}) = \hat{D}^{-1} \left(\sum_{h=1}^H \frac{C_h}{C_h - 1}\sum_{c=1}^{C_h} (e_{hc} - \bar{e}_h)(e_{hc} - \bar{e}_h)^\top\right) \hat{D}^{-1}, \quad \text{ICC} = \frac{\sigma_u^2}{\sigma_u^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2}
$$

# df: a pandas DataFrame holding your selected variables
import numpy as np, statsmodels.api as sm
X = sm.add_constant(df[['x1', 'x2']]).values; y = df["y"].values
d = np.ones(len(y)); t_pop = X.sum(axis=0)
w_cal = d * (1.0 + X @ np.linalg.pinv(X.T @ X) @ (t_pop - X.sum(axis=0)))
fit_greg = sm.WLS(y, X, weights=w_cal).fit(cov_type="HC1")
print(fit_greg.summary())
