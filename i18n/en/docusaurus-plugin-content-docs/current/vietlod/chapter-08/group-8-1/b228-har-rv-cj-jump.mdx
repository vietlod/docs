## 8.1.10.1. Methodological Meaning and Scope of Application

### 8.1.10.1.1. Econometric Foundation and Identification Mechanism

Decomposes total quadratic variation into Barndorff-Nielsen-Shephard bipower continuous variation and discrete jump components across daily, weekly, and monthly Corsi HAR horizons.

### 8.1.10.1.2. Scope of Application and Sample Structure Requirements

High-frequency or realized financial volatility forecasting where diffusive volatility is highly persistent while jump variation mean-reverts rapidly.

- **Mandatory Pre-Estimation Diagnostics**: `arch_lm_heteroskedasticity, adf_unit_root`
- **Mandatory Post-Estimation Diagnostics**: `bns_bipower_jump_decomposition, ljung_box_autocorrelation`

### 8.1.10.1.3. Structural Equations and Asymptotic Covariance Specification

Core structural equation for `har_rv_cj_jump`:

$$
r_t = \mu_t + \varepsilon_t, \quad \varepsilon_t = \sigma_t z_t, \quad \sigma_t^2 = \omega + \sum_{i=1}^q \alpha_i \varepsilon_{t-i}^2 + \sum_{j=1}^p \beta_j \sigma_{t-j}^2
$$

Estimator objective function and sample moment conditions:

$$
\mathbb{E}_t\left[M_{t+1} R_{i,t+1}^e\right] = 0, \quad \mathbb{E}[R_i^e] = \beta_i^\top \lambda
$$

Asymptotic covariance matrix and robust inference metric:

$$
\ell_{\text{QMLE}}(\theta) = -\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t=1}^T \left(\ln \sigma_t^2(\theta) + \frac{\varepsilon_t^2(\theta)}{\sigma_t^2(\theta)}\right)
$$

# df: a pandas DataFrame holding your selected variables
import numpy as np, pandas as pd, statsmodels.api as sm
r = df["y"].values - df["y"].mean()
rv = r**2; bv = (np.pi / 2.0) * np.abs(r) * np.roll(np.abs(r), 1)
j = np.maximum(rv - bv, 0.0); c = np.maximum(rv - j, 0.0)
s_c = pd.Series(c)
H = pd.DataFrame({"y": pd.Series(rv).shift(-1), "cd": s_c, "cw": s_c.rolling(5).mean(), "cm": s_c.rolling(22).mean(), "jd": j}).dropna()
print(sm.OLS(H["y"], sm.add_constant(H[["cd", "cw", "cm", "jd"]])).fit(cov_type="HAC", cov_kwds={"maxlags": 5}).summary())
