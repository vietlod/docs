## 1. Methodological Meaning and Scope of Application

### 1.1. Econometric Foundation and Identification Mechanism

Estimates fractional differencing parameter d in — 0, 1 — to capture slow hyperbolic autocorrelation decay in squared and absolute financial returns. Across empirical econometrics, `figarch` enables researchers to isolate structural parameters while controlling for confounding covariates and verifying identification assumptions via `Stata arch / figarch` in Stata 18 MP, `rugarch` in R 4.x, and `arch` in Python 3.11+.

### 1.2. Scope of Application and Sample Structure Requirements

Financial return series exhibiting persistent long-range volatility memory between stationary GARCH — d=0 — and IGARCH — d=1 . Mandatory post-estimation verification includes testing identification restrictions, evaluating goodness-of-fit metrics, and confirming numerical convergence across all three econometric engines.

### 1.3. Structural Equations and Asymptotic Covariance Specification

Core structural equation for `figarch`:

$$
\phi(L)(1 - L)^d \varepsilon_t^2 = \omega + [1 - \beta(L)]\nu_t \iff \sigma_t^2 = \omega[1 - \beta(1)]^{-1} + \left\{1 - [1 - \beta(L)]^{-1}\phi(L)(1 - L)^d\right\}\varepsilon_t^2
$$

Estimator objective function and sample moment conditions:

$$
\ell_T(\theta) = -\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t=1}^T \left[\ln(2\pi) + \ln(\sigma_t^2(\theta)) + \frac{\varepsilon_t^2}{\sigma_t^2(\theta)}\right], \quad z_t = \frac{\varepsilon_t}{\sigma_t} \sim \text{i.i.d. }(0, 1)
$$

Asymptotic covariance matrix and post-estimation diagnostic metric:

$$
\text{ARCH-LM}(q) = T \cdot R^2_{\hat{z}_t^2 \sim \hat{z}_{t-1}^2,\dots,\hat{z}_{t-q}^2} \xrightarrow{d} \chi^2_q, \quad \text{VaR}_\alpha(t+1 \mid t) = \hat{\mu}_{t+1\mid t} + q_\alpha(z)\hat{\sigma}_{t+1\mid t}
$$
