## 1. Methodological Meaning and Scope of Application

### 1.1. Econometric Foundation and Identification Mechanism

Decomposes regressors into positive and negative partial-sum processes to test short-run and long-run asymmetric pass-through and cointegration. Across empirical econometrics, `nardl` enables researchers to isolate structural parameters while controlling for confounding covariates and verifying identification assumptions via `nardl` in Stata 18 MP, `ARDL` in R 4.x, and `statsmodels` in Python 3.11+.

### 1.2. Scope of Application and Sample Structure Requirements

Mixed I — 0 —/I — 1 — macro-financial time series with strictly no I — 2 — variables testing asymmetric responses to positive versus negative shocks. Mandatory post-estimation verification includes testing identification restrictions, evaluating goodness-of-fit metrics, and confirming numerical convergence across all three econometric engines.

### 1.3. Structural Equations and Asymptotic Covariance Specification

Core structural equation for `nardl`:

$$
\Delta Y_t = \rho Y_{t-1} + \theta^{+\top} X_{t-1}^+ + \theta^{-\top} X_{t-1}^- + \sum_{j=1}^{p-1}\gamma_j \Delta Y_{t-j} + \sum_{j=0}^{q-1}\left(\pi_j^{+\top}\Delta X_{t-j}^+ + \pi_j^{-\top}\Delta X_{t-j}^-\right) + \varepsilon_t
$$

Estimator objective function and sample moment conditions:

$$
\hat{\theta}_T = \arg\max_{\theta \in \Theta} \ell_T\left(Y_1, \dots, Y_T; \theta\right), \quad \Phi(L)(1-L)^d Y_t = \mu + \Theta(L)\varepsilon_t
$$

Asymptotic covariance matrix and post-estimation diagnostic metric:

$$
\text{AICc} = -2\ell_T(\hat{\theta}) + 2k + \frac{2k(k+1)}{T-k-1}, \quad Q_{\text{LB}}(m) = T(T+2)\sum_{\ell=1}^m \frac{\hat{\rho}_\ell^2}{T-\ell} \xrightarrow{d} \chi^2_{m-p-q}
$$
